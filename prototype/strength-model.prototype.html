How does a memory's strength rise and fade?

Each line is one memory. Its strength is worked out from scratch at every moment from three things: the log of times it was used or mentioned (accesses), its significance, and the clock. Above the dashed recall threshold the memory can be recalled. Below it, the memory has faded out of recall. The solid line is the past and the faint line is the future if nothing else happens. The question: does this formula behave the way you'd expect memory to behave, and are the defaults sensible?

The formula

strength = significance boost + max(recent use, lasting floor)

Recent use (ACT-R base level): ln Σ weight × age−fade over counted accesses. Each access fades at its own rate. An access made while the memory is already fresh fades faster (Pavlik & Anderson spacing), so 15 uses in one day mostly wash out.

Lasting floor (Bjork storage strength): grows with the number of accesses at least one day apart and never goes down. When the floor is above the threshold, the memory can no longer fade out of recall.

Closed windows: once an event or ended state's window closes, the close counts as one synthetic access (the recency boost), which then fades normally.

Pinned memories are held just above the threshold.
